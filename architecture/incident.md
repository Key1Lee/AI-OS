# Active operational incidents

Treat `/incident` or evidence of an active, material service or data-impacting event as native, tool-capable incident coordination under `AGENTS.md`. Incident owns **current operational impact, stabilization, recovery coordination, and factual updates**. [Debug](debug.md) owns causal investigation. Safe, authorized mitigation need not wait for root-cause proof. A local failing test, ordinary bug, speculative review risk, planned [migration](migration.md), or routine [release](release.md) is not an incident without active operational impact. An ambiguous alert starts a brief impact check; if no impact is confirmed, report that finding without escalating.

## Assess and stabilize

Establish the affected service and users, observed start window, availability, error/latency or job/backlog symptoms, data integrity and dependency status, and any relevant recent release, migration, or configuration change. Use only available measurements; mark a timestamp or scope unknown when evidence is absent. Check the affected project's runbook and health signals before broad investigation. Choose the safest effective containment from available mechanisms, such as halting a rollout, disabling an unsafe path, or restoring known-good capacity. Check authorization before executing a mitigation; when it is absent, continue safe assessment and present the specific proposed action for approval. Prefer reversible, narrow steps, but do not prescribe a universal mitigation. Preserve transient logs, error examples, deployment identifiers, migration phase and invariant evidence, or relevant state before it disappears when doing so will help recovery or diagnosis. Keep facts, temporal correlations, hypotheses, and confirmed causes distinct.

For a single-user AI-OS, maintain a compact incident note in the conversation or an existing project record when one is available: **impact; recovery state; actions actually taken; evidence with timestamps; useful hypotheses; next operational action; blockers/required authorization**. Add a short timeline only when it improves coordination. Do not invent severity levels, incident roles, precise times, or a new persistent incident store. Reassess impact after each mitigation; if it fails, preserve the result and choose the next safe step rather than repeating a risky action blindly.

Use **ACTIVE** while material impact continues, **MITIGATED** when harm has been reduced but recovery is unproven, **RECOVERING** while relevant indicators move toward normal, and **RESOLVED** only after affected behavior and relevant indicators are restored and independently checked. An alert clearing alone does not prove recovery. Select only pertinent recovery evidence, such as health/smoke checks, successful representative requests, error/latency normalization, queue drain, data freshness, or migration invariants. Keep the observation window bounded by the service's existing criteria or actual risk.

## Authority and handoffs

Read-only investigation, local simulation, staging action, reversible production mitigation, high-impact production action, destructive or irreversible data operation, and security/permission change are distinct. Follow the affected project's policy. `/incident` does not grant production or external-communication authority. For an action needing authorization, state the exact operation, expected effect, and recovery option; obtain authorization unless already explicitly granted. Draft factual updates when useful, separating confirmed impact from uncertain cause; send messages to others only with explicit authorization. Do not expose credentials or unnecessary customer data in evidence.

When impact follows a release, inspect its identity and timing, consider an authorized rollout pause or its existing recovery mechanism, and avoid assuming temporal correlation proves cause. For an active migration, stop unsafe later phases, preserve invariant evidence, and use its defined recovery constraints; never promise rollback across an irreversible step. Send unresolved causal questions to [Debug](debug.md), understood corrective code to Implementation, changed requirements or architecture to Plan, and migration repair to [Migrate](migration.md). After recovery actions, use independent [Verify](verification.md) for restored behavior and [Review](review.md) for durable corrective changes when applicable. Send systemic process or AI-OS gaps to [Audit](../skills/audit/SKILL.md) after stabilization. A concise follow-up identifies impact, actual mitigation and timeline, temporary controls, unresolved cause, and durable work; a full postmortem is a separate request.

## Available evidence and limits

The shared runtime exposes local Qwen health and safe run/audit traces through [runtime](runtime.md) and [router](model-router.md) guidance. Northstar owns its error workflow, `workflow_errors` and effect state, Compose health check, and operator inspection/replay SQL in `projects/Northstar/northstar-leadops/db/002_operations.sql`; replay statements mutate state and require project authorization. Toptal-Testing exposes a loopback `/api/health` for its local app. No shared monitoring, incident-management integration, severity policy, deployment-history service, or production control plane was found. Use project-owned mechanisms as available; do not infer live service health from offline checks or model prose. Add operational scripts only for a demonstrated repeated gap that existing tools cannot safely handle.

`evals/incident-cases.toml` holds 18 routing and twelve response-decision fixtures. In one local Qwen label-only comparison at temperature zero, a basic prompt scored 13/18 routes and 7/12 decisions; a short Incident instruction scored 15/18 and 9/12. The instructed prompt still chose mitigation despite missing production authority and treated a cleared alert as a false alarm without recovery checks. This small stipulated trial supports explicit boundary guidance but does not qualify model-only incident operation: it cannot observe a live event, execute mitigation, establish causality, verify recovery, or establish Codex/Claude behavior. A standalone Skill would add activation and context overhead without operational tool authority or stronger permission enforcement.
